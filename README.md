# SNHU-CS-360-Project-3

**Briefly summarize the requirements and goals of the app you developed. What user needs was this app designed to address?**

The requirements of my app revolve around listing an inventory item to help a user keep track of what is still in stock, while providing a notification, once an item reached zero stock left. This app was meant to help buisinesses, big and small, to keep track of what items they have left to sell.

**What screens and features were necessary to support user needs and produce a user-centered UI for the app? How did your UI designs keep users in mind? Why were your designs successful?**

The screens that were necessary were a log-in screen, a main body screen with the list and addition of new items and numbers, and a setttings screen where the user can log out or change some themes options.

**How did you approach the process of coding your app? What techniques or strategies did you use? How could those techniques or strategies be applied in the future?**

I approached the coding process with an, admittedly, flawed mindset of doing both coding and front-end UI design at the same time, which caused confusion and headaches at times. Once I realized where I was going wrong, I went for a code-now-UI-later approach. In the future, this technique could be used to lessen the stress I could be under and make it much easier to work on my projects.

**How did you test to ensure your code was functional? Why is this process important, and what did it reveal?**

I used the debugging option in the emulator to help me in testing. This process was important to me, because it showed me that real, human, testing can, at times, be better than those made by a program.

**Consider the full app design and development process from initial planning to finalization. Where did you have to innovate to overcome a challenge?**

I had to innovate, when UI items kept snapping into places I didn't want them to be. When this happened, rereading the notes and practices helped me figure out where I was going wrong.

**In what specific component of your mobile app were you particularly successful in demonstrating your knowledge, skills, and experience?**

I was most successful at starting to layout the app's appearance, when drawn on paper. In doing this, I realized I would be much better at physically designing a mock-up and the app's idea, than actually coding it. Even so, I feel as if this project has helped me, greatly, in understanding the back-end side to coding, along with where my strengths and weaknesses lie, when I do code.
